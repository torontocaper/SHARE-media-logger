Responsible investment gains ground in Metropolis pension debate

Trustees, workers and advocates are calling for stronger attention to climate risk, labour standards and long-term value.

By Clark Kent
Published June 4, 2026

Metropolis pension trustees are facing renewed pressure to consider responsible investment policies as workers and community groups ask how long-term savings should respond to climate risk, corporate accountability and human rights concerns.

The discussion follows a series of public meetings where beneficiaries urged pension leaders to clarify how environmental, social and governance risks are considered in investment decisions.

“Responsible investment is not a side issue for pension funds; it is part of the basic duty to understand risk, protect beneficiaries and build long-term value,” said Thomas Kevin, CEO of SHARE.

Supporters say the debate reflects a broader shift in how institutional investors think about fiduciary duty. Critics caution that pension funds should avoid overly prescriptive policies and remain focused on financial performance.

The Metropolis Pension Board is expected to review its investment policy later this summer.
